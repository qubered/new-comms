Comms mockups

Two clickable prototypes for the new comms system. Talk is what a crew member holds; Manager is what the comms person runs. All the names and channels are made up.

TalkThe beltpack. Open this one on your phone. ManagerLive matrix, packs, channels, hardware nodes. Open on a laptop.

Things to try in Talk

  • Pick Stage Manager. Director asks for a PIN, it's 1234.
  • Production is auto: tap it and it latches, hold it and it lets go when you do. Cameras is hold-to-talk. Director is tap on, tap off.
  • Program is a feed you can turn off for yourself. Announce is always on.
  • Levels turns each key into its volume slider. You can't key anything while you're in there.
  • Mic at the bottom kills your mic everywhere and drops anything latched.
  • The gear menu fakes someone talking, or a dropped connection.

Things to try in Manager

  • Live is a grid of packs against channels. Red cells are keyed. It ticks over on its own.
  • Packs edits one pack: PIN, which channels it has, and how each key behaves.
  • Channels shows the direct-line rule (exactly two packs) and where a PGM feed comes from.
  • Hardware nodes lists the bridges. There's nothing to pair; they show up when they connect.

Where the look comes from

The keys are meant to feel like the keys on a Bolero or a Clear-Com pack: a thin colour strip for the channel type, and the whole key going red when your mic is hot. Red is used for nothing else. Everything else is plain: the phone's own font, sentence case, no decoration.

Borrowed from real products: per-channel "someone's talking" indication (Unity, Green-GO), a separate mode for adjusting levels so you can't key a channel by accident (Agent-IC does this with swipe-to-latch), a global mic kill (every soft panel has one), and the matrix view (Riedel Artist, Clear-Com Eclipse).

Things this adds to the spec

None of these change the architecture. Need a yes or no on each.

  • Key heading and sub text. Both set per channel in Manager. The key's behaviour (hold, tap, auto) isn't printed on the key. Agreed.
  • Mic kill. One pack-wide mic off that drops every keyed channel. One extra boolean in the pack's live state.
  • Who's talking, on the key. The Talk app shows the name of whoever is keyed on each channel. Comes straight from the SSE state.
  • Levels is its own mode. Per-channel volume isn't a control inside the key.
  • Direct lines show the other person's name. One channel in Manager, two different key labels.
  • Keys go off while reconnecting. Latched keys drop on connection loss. A short banner says why.
  • Hardware packs are listed but not pickable on the Talk picker.
  • The Live matrix is also the assignment editor. Click a cell to assign. Packs stays for PINs, names and levels.
